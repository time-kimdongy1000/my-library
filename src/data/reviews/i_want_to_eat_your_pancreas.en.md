## 📖 Before Reading

After *At Night, I Become a Monster*, I picked up a second novel by Yoru Sumino: *I Want to Eat Your Pancreas*.

Judging by the title alone, it sounds like a novel about some psychopathic killer. Only after finishing the book do you understand why it was given such a name.

---

## ✍️ After Reading

### Every Person's Day Has the Same Value

There is a conversation in the novel that stayed with me. Haruki asks Sakura, who is losing a little more of her life to illness each day:

“Doesn't it feel like a waste to spend your time here?”

Sakura answers, “Why?” and then explains what she believes.

“You have plenty of things you want to do too, but you aren't trying to do them. You might die tomorrow, you know. A person's time has the same value for everyone. Whatever I choose to do, the value of my day doesn't change.”

We try hard to look away from the word “death.” We live as though tomorrow is guaranteed and each day will keep repeating. That may be why an ordinary day can sometimes feel insignificant.

Yet when people see their own death approaching, they try to live those same days with everything they have. They try to make each one meaningful.

Does that mean a day from before and a day now have different value?

To borrow Sakura's reasoning, the value of a day was one then, and it is still one now. Whatever I do and whatever circumstances I am in, the value of the day remains one. It does not change, and it is equal for everyone.

Being close to death does not make a day itself more valuable.

I am not saying that we should stop trying to live fully, nor am I insisting that we must always try harder. I only mean that a day spent doing nothing and a day spent accomplishing something were both days given to me in equal measure. Perhaps the value of a day is decided less by what I did than by how I choose to receive that day.

### What Would You Do If I Said I Was Truly Afraid to Die?

This is one of Sakura's lines. It moved me so deeply that I even memorized it in Japanese.

私が……私が本当に死ぬのがめちゃくちゃ怖いって言ったら、どうする？

*What would you do if I told you... if I told you I was really, terribly afraid to die?*

As the story unfolds, Sakura often seems like a girl who barely worries about death. Regardless of how her condition progresses, she eats whatever she wants and goes wherever she wants. To anyone watching, she appears to live like an ordinary high school student.

The truth is different.

What keeps her ordinary life going is the collection of medicine and syringes in her bag. She depends on medicine to carry her through each day, and even she admits that she is afraid of the death drawing closer to her.

How would I act if I, or someone precious to me, were losing a little more life every day?

Haruki cannot find an answer.

I wondered what I would say. No ordinary words or gestures could fully express it. This is not something happening to me, but it is Sakura's reality.

Without saying anything, Haruki chooses to lie down beside her. I think I would lie beside her too, but I would also want to say something.

“I'll do everything I can to help you hold on to your honest feelings and your ordinary life, whatever that takes.”

Perhaps those are the kind of words Sakura wanted to hear. From the time she first met Haruki, I think she saw him as someone who could help her preserve both her truth and her everyday life through the time she had left.

### A Day's Value

This book made me think about what gives a day its value.

There have been days when I came home, exercised diligently, and studied Japanese. There have also been days when I came home and did nothing but play games. Exercising did not make one day inherently more valuable. It may have helped my health, but spending another day gaming did not make that day worth less.

Perhaps choosing to play games was also the choice that the person I was that day needed in order to spend it well.

In the end, a day's value does not change according to whether I played games or exercised. What matters may be accepting what kind of day I wanted at that moment and what choice I made, instead of measuring the day only by what I accomplished.

### A Person Who Changes

This is also a book that changed me.

As I read it, I thought I resembled Haruki. I was not particularly interested in other people either. Whatever happened around me, I simply carried on with my own work. I am not sure that condition has been completely cured even now.

Sometimes I speak energetically with other people. On other days, I am drawn endlessly inward and want nothing more than to talk with myself.

After reading this book, however, I decided to change a little and took one step beyond the borders of my own world.

Taking that step has often been painful. When I was alone, it was enough for me to be content. The closer I came to other people, the more conflicts and disagreements I had to face. Perhaps I had chosen solitude because I was afraid of those very collisions.

But I cannot remain inside the cave forever.

Haruki knows he may be condemned, yet he still gives Kyoko the *Living with Dying* journal and tries to become her friend. In the same way, moving forward means stepping beyond the circle I have drawn around my own world instead of remaining safely inside it.

It is like the bird in *Demian* breaking through the egg that surrounds it and emerging into a new world.

---

## 🌙 Closing Thoughts

Once again, Yoru Sumino shook something in my heart. After reading this book, I too became a little more capable of stepping beyond the world I had kept to myself.

In the novel, Haruki asks Sakura:

“What does living mean to you?”

I still do not know the exact answer to that question.

But this book left me with at least one thought. However I spent today, it was a day I would only ever have once. And even when I am afraid of colliding with other people, stepping outside my own world is also part of the process of living.

Then what does “living” mean to me?

I think that is an answer I will have to keep searching for as I live the rest of my life.
